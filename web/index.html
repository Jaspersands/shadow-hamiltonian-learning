Learning a Hamiltonian from classical shadows

A small Heisenberg chain with hidden couplings Jij and fields hi is prepared at finite temperature. Each measurement records one bitstring in a product Pauli basis. From enough of these snapshots we estimate the local observables, invert the thermal state to recover (J, h), and follow a coupling as it drifts. Choosing the bases deliberately rather than at random, and correcting for asymmetric readout errors, both improve the estimate.

Method
  • Model. H = Σi<j Jij(XX + YY + ZZ)ij + Σi hiZi and ρ = e−βH/Z, built as dense matrices.
  • Snapshots. Rotate ρ into a product Pauli basis and sample one bitstring from its diagonal. Readout errors then flip bits with probabilities p(1|0) and p(0|1).
  • Estimators. Random bases: the average of ∏ 3(−1)b over snapshots whose basis matches the observable. Chosen bases: the plain mean over matching snapshots. With readout errors each (−1)b becomes g(b) = ((Mᵀ)⁻¹(1, −1))b, which is unbiased even when the two error rates differ.
  • Choosing bases. Huang, Kueng and Preskill's greedy algorithm: for each snapshot and qubit, pick the Pauli that most reduces a confidence bound on the worst-estimated observable (ε = 0.9, with weights kept in log space so long runs do not underflow).
  • Inversion. BFGS on Σo(⟨o⟩ρ(J,h) − ô)² in a Web Worker. The Python package differentiates exactly through e−βH, a formula that stays finite when levels are degenerate (as they are for any SU(2)-symmetric chain). The condition number is the ratio of the largest to the smallest singular value of ∂⟨o⟩/∂(J, h) at the true parameters; a large value means some combination of parameters is hard to see.
  • Tracking. The state is (J₀₁, h₀, h₁) with random-walk process noise. Each snapshot gives one ±1 outcome per compatible observable, and these outcomes are correlated. The filter makes one joint update with their exact covariance ⟨oaob⟩ − ⟨oa⟩⟨ob⟩ and updates the covariance in Joseph form. It is a Gaussian approximation to a discrete measurement, which becomes accurate once many snapshots have been absorbed.
