Learn a Hamiltonian from classical shadows

A Heisenberg chain with unknown couplings Jij and fields hi is prepared in its Gibbs state; the page measures it with randomised or derandomised Pauli shadows (optionally with readout errors), estimates the Pauli observables, and recovers (J, h) by gradient descent through the Gibbs state — then tracks a drifting coupling with an extended Kalman filter. Nothing here is a canned curve.

How this page computes its numbers
  • Model. H = Σi<j Jij(XiXj+YiYj+ZiZj) + Σi hiZi as a dense 2ᴺ×2ᴺ matrix; ρβ = e−βH/Z from a Jacobi eigendecomposition.
  • Shadows. Each snapshot rotates ρ into a product Pauli basis (H for X, H·S† for Y) and samples a bitstring from the diagonal; readout errors flip bits with p(1|0), p(0|1).
  • Estimators. Random bases: ∏ 3(−1)bδ(W,P) averaged over all snapshots. Derandomised bases: mean of ∏(−1)b over matching snapshots. With readout errors, (−1)b → g(b) = ((Mᵀ)⁻¹(1,−1))b, unbiased for asymmetric errors.
  • Derandomisation. Huang–Kueng–Preskill Algorithm 1: per shot and qubit choose the Pauli minimising Σo e−ε²ho/2[1 − ν·1[compatible]·3−remaining](1 − ν/3w)M−m−1, ε = 0.9.
  • Inversion. BFGS with central-difference gradients on Σo(⟨o⟩ρ(J,h) − ô)²; the Python package uses JAX gradients through eigh. The condition number is the ratio of extreme singular values of ∂⟨o⟩/∂(J,h) at the truth.
  • EKF. State (J₀₁, h₀, h₁), random-walk process noise, observation z = ±1 outcome of each compatible model observable, linearised through the Gibbs state, R = 1 − ⟨o⟩².
